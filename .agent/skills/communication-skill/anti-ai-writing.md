# Anti-AI Writing Reference

## Purpose

This file supports `communication-skill.md`.

It identifies common AI-generated writing patterns so they can be removed or rewritten.

Use this as a reference, not as a strict banned-word list.

---

These words are not always wrong, but repeated use can make writing sound artificial:

- leverage
- optimize
- streamline
- transform
- revolutionize
- elevate
- empower
- accelerate
- facilitate
- enhance
- unlock
- maximize
- utilize
- robust
- dynamic
- scalable
- innovative
- cutting-edge
- future-proof
- seamless
- impactful
- meaningful
- transformative
- actionable

## Common AI Sentence Patterns

Avoid overusing:

- Not just X, but Y
- Whether you're X, Y, or Z
- The key to X is Y
- By doing X, you can Y
- It is important to note that
- It is worth noting that
- In today's fast-paced world
- In the modern business landscape
- From X to Y
- X is more than just Y

---

## Common AI Transitions

AI often overuses:

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- Additionally
- Consequently
- Therefore
- Thus
- Hence
- Nevertheless
- Nonetheless
- Similarly
- Likewise
- In contrast
- On the other hand
- With that said

Prefer simpler transitions:

- Also
- But
- So
- Still
- Yet
- Anyway

---

## Weak Introductions

Avoid generic starts:

- In today's fast-paced world...
- Technology is rapidly evolving...
- Businesses face many challenges...
- AI is transforming industries...
- Success requires hard work...
- Communication is more important than ever...

Better starts use specifics:

> Last year our support team closed 8,000 tickets. This year we are on pace for 12,000.

---

## Weak Conclusions

Avoid generic endings:

- In conclusion...
- To summarize...
- Ultimately...
- At the end of the day...
- The future is bright.
- The possibilities are endless.
- Success requires dedication.

Better endings give a next step, decision, recommendation, or specific takeaway.

## AI Email Habits

Common AI openings:

- I hope this email finds you well.
- I hope you're doing well.
- I trust you're having a great week.
- I wanted to reach out regarding.
- I wanted to follow up on.

Common AI closings:

- Please do not hesitate to reach out.
- Let me know if you have any questions.
- Thank you for your time and consideration.
- I look forward to hearing from you.

These are not wrong, but use them only when appropriate.

---

## AI LinkedIn Habits

Watch for:

- Fake vulnerability
- Fake dramatic stories
- Generic lessons
- Engagement bait

Examples:

- I'll be honest...
- Nobody talks about this.
- Here's the truth.
- Three years ago I made a mistake.
- What are your thoughts?
- Agree or disagree?
- Let me know in the comments.

---

## AI YouTube Script Habits

Common hooks:

- What if I told you...
- Imagine if...
- Here's something nobody talks about...
- By the end of this video...
- You're making a huge mistake if...

Common retention phrases:

- But here's the thing...
- That's not all...
- It gets even better...
- Here's where it gets interesting...
- The real secret is...

Use only when they fit naturally.

## What Human Writing Does Differently

Human writing often includes:

- Specific examples
- Uneven rhythm
- Real observations
- Personal context
- Small imperfections
- Tangents
- Humor
- Frustration
- Concrete details
- Unexpected phrasing

Human writing is not perfect. It is specific.

---

## Human Alternatives

AI:

> This powerful solution empowers teams to streamline collaboration and drive meaningful results.

Human:

> This cut our Monday meeting from one hour to fifteen minutes.

AI:

> In today's fast-paced digital environment, businesses must embrace innovation to remain competitive.

Human:

> The team next door is doing the same work with half the staff. That is why everyone is paying attention.

AI:

> By implementing this framework, organizations can maximize productivity while reducing inefficiencies.

Human:

> We started using this process in January. By March, the team had stopped missing deadlines.

---

## Final Anti-AI Check

Ask:

1. Could this apply to almost any topic?
2. Does this sound like a generic business article?
3. Are there too many smooth transitions?
4. Are all sections too balanced?
5. Are there too many three-item lists?
6. Are there unsupported adjectives?
7. Is there at least one real detail?

If the answer suggests the writing is generic, rewrite it with specifics.
